# ForgeTax October restoration: evidence and limits

Checked 2026-10-12. Target is Steam app **5245810**, GraveYardDev’s desktop ForgeTax, incorporating October 6–10 announcements. This document distinguishes developer-confirmed mechanics, first-person playing reports, community-reported code analysis, and explicit approximation. It is **not** a claim of complete or byte-identical original-game restoration.

## Source hierarchy

Official Steam announcements take priority over earlier guides. The readable originals can also be retrieved with [Steam News API](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=5245810&count=100&maxlength=0). `forgetax.net` is a browser fan recreation and is excluded as a source.

`forgetax.tools` is a different, unofficial database. Its [forging page](https://www.forgetax.tools/guides/forging-odds) explicitly says its numbers come from the game’s code/economy table; its footer describes effects/forms/odds as worked out from that code. It does **not** supply a verifiable extraction procedure, original source files, or a version hash. Therefore its tables below are **community-reported, medium confidence**, not independently verified datamining. Its starter list still contains Leather Loop, contradicting the later October 7 official update; the implementation follows the official update instead.

## First-person original-play evidence

Aaron’s October 7 [beginner walkthrough](https://grindnstrat.com/forgetax-beginner-guide-and-walkthrough-controls-tips/) describes playing the Steam game, physical controls and concrete results. It supports persistent one-time heat, +0 having no break, 1000-ticket draw, Success +1/Great +2/Damage −1, charge on the sword, four-second Frenzy, three daily relic candidates, one deposited sword per credit regardless of value, eight slots, discard without refund, tribute retrieval before the bell, and **full offered value returned as gold even on failure**. Tax checkpoints 1800/7500/45800/232000 agree with the community table. These are high-to-medium confidence playing reports, not developer specification.

The same walkthrough says merchant starts day 3 then repeats every three days. The official calendar screenshot has scale marks at April 17/20/23/26/29, independently consistent with that cadence. The [all-stock official achievement](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/5245810/achievements/) confirms finite inventory, but does not reveal its exact counts or prices.

## Centralized community parameters

All balance definitions reside in `src/rules.ts`; `simulation.ts` owns transitions, physical locations, shops and restoration.

- [Tribute table](https://www.forgetax.tools/tribute): all 21 quota values and repeated rounded ×1.2 endless growth. Official confirmation covers day 21 and unchanged first three, not every intermediate value.
- [Forging odds](https://www.forgetax.tools/guides/forging-odds): raw 9/2/5/12/2 tickets, break curve, multiplicative mitigation and 1000-ticket normalization. +5 29.7%/+10 46.7% are also in the playing guide. The published +0 table says 572/95/238/95 while its described largest-remainder algorithm yields a one-ticket difference; this implementation uses the stated algorithm and does not claim exact per-ticket identity.
- [Sword value](https://www.forgetax.tools/guides/sword-value): normal base 320, special base 640, enhancement ×1.5, Great permanent factor 2.5/2.25, sword-held flat/multiplier effects.
- [Frenzy](https://www.forgetax.tools/guides/frenzy): base interval 640 ms; normal/Frenzy relic speed bonuses; four-second duration; Wick +2 seconds and Red Hourglass +1. The playing guide’s approximate seven blows in four seconds does not establish a different base interval. Heat animation time is a UI approximation, not part of this numeric model.
- [Merchant](https://www.forgetax.tools/merchant): basic quantities by visit, special sword grade, tax-relative pricing, repeat purchase ×3, consumable minimum day/stock/effects, delivery and once-per-run incense. Minimum item prices are provisional fallback values rather than independently verified values. Legendary sword variant pricing is not implemented.
- [Relic definitions](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics): effect, credit cost and minimum day; [achievement mappings](https://www.forgetax.tools/achievements): family reward and run-feat gates. Official changes override stale descriptions.
- [Relic shop](https://www.forgetax.tools/tools/relic-shop): rarity-first draw without replacement, recovery relic weight ×1.75 during days 2–6, reroll price 15% of base quota with minimum 150 and ×2.5 per **paid** reroll. Free rerolls do not increase that counter. It publishes day-6 rarity 70/25/5; this build provisionally uses that distribution on other days too. Exact day-specific rarity tables and late-game value-relief weights remain unverified.

## Explicit approximation and coverage

Starting gold is provisionally zero. Ordinary supply now follows the community-reported [evolution tree](https://www.forgetax.tools/swords/evolution): shuffled groups of three supply lanes, with a fixed 50/50 final branch in each lane. Merchant special family selection remains uniform and its exact original distribution is not established. See [sword-evolution.md](sword-evolution.md) for the video cross-check, form thresholds, persistent appearance and remaining evidence limits. Base heat remains binary, and full next-hit Frenzy begins at ten charges. Taking a Frenzy blade off the anvil ends its active window in this adaptation; the first-person guide confirms saved charge, not this exact timer behavior. Paid tribute does not reduce anger automatically. Relic display icons, names translated here, and physical placement defaults are adaptation assets.

The database lists 98 relics, including four disabled entries, hence 94 currently active definitions. This build implements **94 functional relics**, including every effect explicitly itemized in the audited October announcements, all nine family mastery rewards and seven run-feat rewards. The remaining 37 were added with community-reported behavior, not presented as developer-confirmed data. Conditional chains use per-sword counters/tickets, value bonus records, protection shields and one-time milestone sets; supply and credit relics update real resources.

Exact combination ordering is not exposed by these descriptions. Specifically `lastBonus` for [Split Twin Mirror](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics/split-twin-mirror) records the last processed base/flat/multiplier relic bonus in this model; source descriptions do not specify priority when several bonuses fire. [Bound Idol](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics/bound-idol) tracks each crossed four-level milestone once; handling a multi-level jump across several milestones is inferred. [Giant’s Spine](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics/giants-spine) prevents its converted damage from charging a new shield, but the priority when multiple shields are equipped is inferred. [Headless Bell](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics/headless-bell) triggers on natural timer expiry, not taking the sword away. [Smith’s Testament](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics/smiths-testament) uses the highest delivered grade with full payment. [Collector’s Hook](https://www.forgetax.tools/relics/collectors-hook) is once per day after any reroll, and the bonus is applied only during a separate sword deposit. Purchases cannot deposit swords implicitly. The Great/flat interaction follows the reported permanent 2.5/2.25 multiplier, while exact original effect sequencing remains unverified. These are specific remaining fidelity boundaries rather than absent mechanics.

Inspection Stamp adds 100 to the **flat component**, before the multiplier; its translated card now states that distinction. The [reported value formula](https://www.forgetax.tools/guides/sword-value) explicitly lists Stamp under flat effects. Thus a multiplier can make the final value change larger than 100.

Four disabled definitions (Copper Ledger, Little Strongbox, Burial Coin, Relief Sack) are excluded. The finite shop cannot offer owned or locked definitions. The nine sword families now use eleven two-grade visual stages, including common early ordinary shapes and intermediate supply-lane shapes. Appearance follows the highest achieved level, while current enhancement still controls value and odds. A damage result preserves form; rewind restores the earlier form. Procedural geometry and concise Chinese names are adaptations, not original mesh assets or a claim of exact translations. Fishblade’s Kraken Tentacle is a stage, not a separate supply family.

## Save and verification

Version 2 serializes physical sword locations, one hand/one anvil/three racks, value modifiers, per-sword tickets, pending inheritance, merchant sold quantities/prices, shop credits/free rerolls, consumable charges, permanent run tickets and discoveries/unlocks. Undo snapshots are intentionally not saved; loading cannot rewind an old economic transaction. Earlier v2 saves missing newly added fields receive zero/false defaults without gifts or resource grants; explicitly malformed fields are still rejected. Version 1 migrates progress to the current quota table, clamps day 1–21 and disables already-consumed starter gifts; it is a compatibility bridge, not evidence of original-game accuracy.

Simulation tests exercise cold/anvil prerequisites, continuous heat, deck sums, Great/cap, Frenzy boundary, visit stock, tribute retrieval/refund, three failures/final clear/endless, shop resource atomicity/slots, conditional tickets/inheritance, ash per sword, consumable/transaction rewind, NaN/malformed saves, and seeded mixed physical/economic actions with a save/restore after every step. Integration save-slot/input tests are maintained by the parent lane. Browser visual verification is separate.

A reproducible seeded strategy check ran 300 contracts with actual model actions and restored every settlement: buy at most two merchant basic blades, prioritize available affordable recovery relics while leaving two blades, and forge each remaining blade toward 85% of the unpaid quota. It cleared 61/300 runs (20.3%), averaging final day 16.19. A deliberately naive no-shop strategy cleared 0/100 and averaged day 10.26. This shows the implemented purchasing/recovery loop can complete the contract; it is not an original-game win-rate measurement or a balancing equivalence claim. No probabilities were changed to obtain these results.

## Purchasing station and sword coins — transaction correction

The [official Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5245810/ForgeTax/) says that the relic machine takes swords. Its [original screenshot](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/5245810/e23a97a75b7d857f5565dad480f62f6cf3018f5f/ss_e23a97a75b7d857f5565dad480f62f6cf3018f5f.1920x1080.jpg?t=1791135247) shows three display cases with sword-symbol prices, a sword physically entering the slot, an arrow toward a sword ×1 symbol, and a separate sword-symbol `00` balance. The lower-right reroll price is explicitly denominated in G, distinct from that balance. The local reference `work/forge-tax-original-2.jpg` was SHA-256 matched to this official Steam screenshot fetched via the store app-details API. These observations confirm a purchasing machine with a deposit boundary, not the player's owned-relic display shelf. The screenshot alone does not establish a physical coin pickup/drop action, and none is invented here.

The user calls the stored purchasing balance **剑币**. We use that term in the presentation and action errors, while preserving the compatible save field `state.swordCredits`; `game.swordCoins` is a read-only alias. `depositSword` removes one actual sword and separately increments the balance (ordinary blades give one, irrespective of enhancement/value; Collector's Hook's documented once-per-day exception gives two). `buyRelic` spends **only already deposited coins**; supplying a nonempty sword-ID argument fails without changing any resource. Gold is unaffected by deposit/purchase, and the free starter choice continues through `claimFreeRelic` without charging either currency.

This corrects the earlier implementation's direct selected-sword payment shortcut. The first-person playing report and community Hook description support the numeric exchange; terminology and the internal credit representation are adaptation choices, not a claim that every original interface or internal data structure has been reproduced exactly. Regression coverage verifies atomic insufficient-balance/bypass failures, enhanced-sword parity, repeated deposit rejection, coin persistence across days and browser storage, independent coin/gold balances in separate slots, and free-gift clearing without currency charges.

The model now persists `shop.displayIds` for each round’s fixed case positions, separately from the still-purchasable `shop.ids`. Buying the middle case leaves the other two positions unchanged; `game.relicOffers` returns definitions/null in the original slot order, so the sold case stays empty until a reroll or a new day. The starter choice clears all gift cases. Older v2 saves without slot metadata initialize it from their available IDs; previously sold positions cannot be reconstructed from those older saves. Explicit duplicate/unknown slots or available IDs outside the slots are rejected. This is a fixed-display adaptation consistent with the official three-case purchasing station; the screenshot does not independently establish every post-purchase animation. Prices and purchasable selection are unchanged.
